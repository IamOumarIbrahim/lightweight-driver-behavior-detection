\begin{abstract}
Camera-based driver monitoring must balance detection accuracy, false alarms, and latency under subject and illumination variation. We introduce a reproducible benchmark of three nano-scale detectors---YOLO11n, YOLO26n, and D-FINE-N---on subject-disjoint visible (RGB) and near-infrared (NIR) tracks derived from DMD and Drive\&Act. The protocol fixes $640\times640$ input, a 220-epoch budget, effective batch size 32, validation-only threshold selection, and confirmation-gated test access. The completed RGB YOLO comparison covers three seeds per architecture. YOLO11n reaches $91.79\pm0.44\%$ mAP@0.5, $0.8660\pm0.0049$ micro-F1, and $68.0\pm1.3$ FPS, while YOLO26n improves the stricter mAP@0.5:0.95 to $56.98\pm0.81\%$ with fewer parameters and FLOPs. This establishes a measurable throughput--localization trade-off on negative-heavy continuous data. D-FINE-N and controlled NIR 1:2 versus 1:6 training-negative results will be inserted after their checksum-frozen runs complete; no interim value is used as a final result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Driver distraction and fatigue are safety-critical states, yet their appearance varies strongly across drivers, cabins, cameras, and illumination. Driver-monitoring research spans physiological, vehicle, and vision signals; cameras are attractive because they are non-contact and can localize visible behaviors, but dependable deployment also requires low latency and few nuisance alerts \cite{dong2011review,michelaraki2023realtime}.

Public driver datasets provide complementary evidence. DMD contains multimodal attention and alertness recordings across face, body, and hand views \cite{ortega2020dmd}, whereas Drive\&Act provides color, infrared, depth, and pose streams for fine-grained in-cabin activity \cite{martin2019driveact}. Existing reports are difficult to compare when subject identity leaks across splits, background frames are discarded, confidence thresholds are tuned on test data, or latency boundaries differ.

This work addresses these issues through three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a subject-disjoint RGB/NIR benchmark that retains negative frames and reports false alarms alongside COCO AP;
    \item an equal-budget comparison of two YOLO nano detectors and the nano D-FINE real-time DETR; and
    \item a public, checksum-backed workflow that separates training, validation, and one protected test pass while keeping licensed media and checkpoints local.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
YOLO formulates detection as direct image-to-box regression and established the accuracy--speed motivation for one-stage detectors \cite{redmon2016yolo}. DETR instead treats detection as set prediction with bipartite matching, eliminating anchors and non-maximum suppression (NMS) \cite{carion2020detr}. RT-DETR adapted this end-to-end paradigm to real-time operation through an efficient hybrid encoder and query selection \cite{zhao2024rtdetr}. D-FINE improves DETR localization by iteratively refining box-edge distributions and applying localization self-distillation \cite{peng2025dfine}; its distributional view is related to generalized focal localization \cite{li2020gfl}.

For driver monitoring, DMD emphasizes attention and alertness under real and simulated driving \cite{ortega2020dmd}, while Drive\&Act emphasizes fine-grained actions and cross-view/modal variation \cite{martin2019driveact}. Our objective is not a new architecture. It is controlled comparison: identical data membership, budget, evaluation code, and profiling boundaries across lightweight architectural families.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{RGB Track}
The RGB track contains 15,723 $640\times640$ face crops sampled at 1 FPS from 81 DMD recordings of 14 drivers. Four mutually exclusive cues are annotated: \texttt{phone\_use}, \texttt{drinking}, \texttt{yawning}, and \texttt{hand\_over\_mouth}. There are 3,001 positive and 12,722 negative frames. Exhaustive subject assignment selects an 8:3:3 split by minimizing worst class-proportion deviation, with RMSE as tie-breaker. Table~\ref{tab:rgbdata} gives the frozen counts.

\subsection{NIR Track and Ratio Ablation}
The NIR annotation pool contains 3,763 one-second snippets from 30 active-NIR ($850$ nm) Drive\&Act streams and 15 subjects. Each selected snippet is represented by ten frames at 10 FPS. Source offsets $2,5,\ldots,29$ are cropped to the driver region and resized to $640\times640$; bounding boxes are linearly interpolated at $0.1$-s intervals. The ontology is \texttt{drinking} and \texttt{phone\_use}.

The 9:3:3 subject split fixes 881 validation snippets (120 positive) and 850 test snippets (111 positive). Only training negatives vary: both conditions retain 270 positive snippets, with 540 negatives for 1:2 and 1,620 for 1:6. The 1:2 negatives are a subject-stratified, seed-13 deterministic subset of the 1:6 pool. Thus the full conditions contain 2,541 and 3,621 snippets, respectively. NIR uses seed 13 only; it is a ratio ablation rather than a multi-seed experiment.

\subsection{Models and Training Control}
We compare Ultralytics YOLO11n, YOLO26n, and D-FINE-N. All runs use $640\times640$ input, physical batch size 8, four-step gradient accumulation, 220 epochs, automatic mixed precision, and no early stopping. The final incomplete accumulation window is normalized by its true sample count. Official pinned model-native optimization is retained: Ultralytics resolves \texttt{optimizer=auto} with a linear schedule (\texttt{cos\_lr=false}), while D-FINE uses AdamW and its MultiStepLR schedule. RGB uses seeds 13, 37, and 73; NIR uses seed 13 for each ratio.

\subsection{Evaluation and Profiling}
COCO mAP@0.5:0.95 and mAP@0.5 follow the standard COCO evaluator \cite{lin2014coco}. A threshold $\tau^*$ is selected on validation by maximum micro-F1, then higher precision and higher threshold. At IoU 0.5, detections are greedily matched one-to-one within class. We report
\begin{equation}
F_1=\frac{2PR}{P+R}, \qquad
\mathrm{FAR}=100\frac{\mathrm{FP}_{\mathrm{negative\ frames}}}{N_{\mathrm{negative\ frames}}}.
\end{equation}
The checkpoint, validation predictions, and $\tau^*$ are checksum-frozen before a single test pass. Batch-1 FP16 profiling on an RTX 4060 reports synchronized model-forward and tensor-to-final-detections boundaries, excluding disk I/O, decode, preprocessing, and metrics.

Per-class AP supports the same interpretation. \section{Limitations and Completion Criteria}
RGB and NIR are different datasets and ontologies, so their results should not be interpreted as a paired spectral transfer test. NIR uses one seed and cannot quantify stochastic variance. Interpolated NIR boxes may miss rapid non-linear motion, and RTX 4060 latency does not transfer directly to other accelerators. Finally, the single-cue ontology excludes concurrent behaviors.

This draft does not invent missing results. Final cross-model and cross-spectral claims require RGB D-FINE-N seeds 37/73, validation and protected testing for all three D-FINE-N seeds, and all six NIR model-ratio runs. The repository records those completion criteria and generates publication artifacts only after validation manifests are frozen.

\section{Conclusion}
The completed RGB YOLO benchmark demonstrates that model selection depends on the deployment objective: YOLO11n provides the stronger real-time operating point and YOLO26n the stronger strict-localization AP. Subject-disjoint partitions, retained negatives, FAR, fixed profiling boundaries, and validation-only threshold calibration make this comparison more deployment-relevant than AP alone. The same protocol is prepared for D-FINE-N and the NIR training-negative ablation; final conclusions will be updated after those declared runs complete.
